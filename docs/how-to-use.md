# How to Use These Curricula

These curricula are flexible. You can use them in a classroom, at home, or in a community setting without needing long lectures or complicated preparation.

## Common ways to use them

### Classroom

Use one curriculum as a short weekly module that fits into advisory time, social studies, technology time, or a life-skills block.

### Homeschool

Integrate a curriculum into social studies, digital citizenship, or practical life-skills work.

### After school

Run lessons as a discussion group, enrichment club, or community learning activity.

## A typical lesson rhythm

A simple weekly format might look like this:

1. **Lesson 1 — Core concept** (10–20 minutes)
2. **Lesson 2 — Reinforcement discussion** (10–20 minutes)
3. **Lesson 3 — Extension activity**

This rhythm keeps lessons short and approachable while giving students time to revisit ideas.

## Teaching approach

The goal is conversation and exploration, not memorization.

You do not need to be an expert to use these materials. In many cases, the best teaching move is to ask a question, invite examples, and let students explain what they notice.

Because the curricula are modular, you can use a full sequence or choose just the lessons that fit your learners.
