# Printable Information Organization Cards

Simple cards you can copy, print, or rewrite by hand. Keep them nearby — a step you can actually find in the moment beats a beautiful chart you can't. Cut them out, tape them inside a notebook, or keep them where the sorting happens.

:::caution Everyday organization skills, not a replacement for support
These cards teach everyday information organization skills. They are not therapy, disability evaluation, academic intervention, legal advice, medical advice, or a replacement for trusted adults, educators, caregivers, accommodations, or qualified support. Kids should not be treated as lazy or broken when information feels messy or hard to organize. If information feels consistently inaccessible, unsafe, private, sensitive, or overwhelming, asking a trusted adult for support is part of the system.
:::

---

## Information Organization Loop Card

> **THE INFORMATION ORGANIZATION LOOP**
>
> 1. What information do I have?
> 2. What am I trying to use it for?
> 3. What belongs together?
> 4. What labels would help future-me?
> 5. What is an example?
> 6. What is a non-example?
> 7. What relationships, order, or patterns can I show?
> 8. What source or location should I track?
> 9. How will I find this again?

---

## Quick Organization Card

> **WHEN THE PILE FEELS BIG**
>
> - Gather it.
> - Sort it.
> - Label it.
> - Compare examples.
> - Show connections.
> - Track the source.
> - Make it findable.

---

## First Sort Card

> **TURN THE PILE INTO GROUPS**
>
> - What information do I have?
> - What groups do I notice?
> - What does not fit yet?
> - What is missing?
> - What group should I look at first?

---

## Category Check Card

> **CATEGORIES ARE CHOICES**
>
> - What is my purpose?
> - What groups would help?
> - Is anything in the wrong group?
> - Is one category too big?
> - Do I need a new category?
> - Would another sort answer the question better?

---

## Future-You Label Card

> **NAME IT SO YOU CAN FIND IT**
>
> - Will I know what this is later?
> - Does the label say the topic?
> - Does it say the type of thing?
> - Does it need a date or version?
> - Is it short enough to use?
> - Would someone else understand it?

## Choose the Shape Card

> **PICK THE RIGHT ORGANIZER**
>
> - Need to show order? Use a timeline.
> - Need to compare? Use a table.
> - Need to show parts? Use a diagram.
> - Need to show steps? Use a flowchart.
> - Need to show relationships? Use a concept map.
> - Need to show cause/effect? Use arrows.

---

## Ask for Support Card

> **SOME INFORMATION NEEDS A TRUSTED ADULT**
>
> Ask a trusted adult for support if:
>
> - the information feels impossible to organize again and again
> - you cannot access what you need
> - instructions or sources are unclear
> - information is private, sensitive, medical, legal, or unsafe
> - you feel ashamed or scared to ask
> - the problem feels bigger than everyday organization
>
> *Asking for support can be part of the system.*

---

Information organization tools help with everyday sorting, labeling, note-taking, source tracking, diagrams, and finding information again. They do not replace trusted adults, educators, caregivers, accommodations, medical care, legal advice, therapy, or qualified support when information feels consistently inaccessible, unsafe, private, sensitive, or overwhelming.

These cards pair with the eight toolkit lessons, starting with [Sort Before You Solve](./lesson-01-sort-before-you-solve.md).
