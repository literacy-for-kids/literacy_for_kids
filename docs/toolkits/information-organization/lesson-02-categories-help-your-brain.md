# Categories Help Your Brain

**Short summary:** Categories help your brain group information, but the best categories depend on the purpose.

## Big idea

A category is a group of things that belong together in some way — and categories are **chosen**, not discovered. The same item can belong in different categories depending on the question you are asking.

## Why it matters

Useful categories make thinking easier: they shrink a big mess into a few understandable groups. But bad categories can hide important information, and a category that is too big stops helping. Knowing that categories are choices means you can change them when they stop working.

## Kid-friendly explanation

A store puts similar things near each other so people can find them. But a store could organize by food type, by meal, by color, by price, or by temperature. None of those is the one "true" way — the best category depends on what people need to find.

## Tool: the Category Check

> - What is my purpose?
> - What groups would help?
> - Is anything in the wrong group?
> - Is one category too big?
> - Do I need a new category?
> - Would another sort answer the question better?

## Activity: Same Stuff, Different Categories

Use a set of cards: apple, chips, water bottle, sandwich, cookie, carrots, soup. Sort them:

- food / drink
- healthy sometimes / healthy often
- needs a fridge / does not need a fridge
- crunchy / not crunchy

Then discuss how the purpose changed the categories — same stuff, different sorts.

## Discussion questions

- Why are categories useful?
- Can one thing belong in more than one category?
- When can a category be unfair or misleading?
- How do you know when a category is too big?

## Try it this week

Take one list and sort it two different ways. Notice what each sort helps you see.

## Adult note

Categories are powerful. Help kids notice that categories can clarify — but they can also oversimplify or label people unfairly. Avoid using people as sorting examples unless the lesson is carefully framed and safe.
