# Track Sources and Evidence

**Short summary:** When information comes from somewhere, tracking the source helps you check it, credit it, and find it again.

## Big idea

A source is where information came from. Evidence is information that supports a claim. Claims, sources, and evidence should stay connected — if you lose the source, it is much harder to check anything later.

## Why it matters

Source tracking helps with media, civic, legal, health, environmental, and school research. Not every source is equally useful for every question — and knowing *where* a fact came from is what lets you decide how much to trust it. Private or personal information needs extra care.

## Kid-friendly explanation

A source tracker leaves breadcrumbs back to where information came from. Without the breadcrumbs, you may remember the fact but never find the path again — and then you cannot check it, share it, or defend it.

## Tool: Claim / Source / Evidence / Question

> - Claim: ___
> - Source: ___
> - Evidence: ___
> - What this source helps answer: ___
> - Question I still have: ___
> - Can I find it again? ___

## Activity: Keep the Claim Connected

Give kids fictional claims and sources. Have them match each claim with its source, evidence, and an open question. Then show one claim that *lost* its source — discuss why that matters and what future-you can no longer do with it.

## Discussion questions

- Why does source tracking matter?
- What happens when a claim gets separated from its source?
- How can a source help one question but not another?
- What information should stay private?

## Try it this week

When you write down one fact, also write where it came from.

## Adult note

Source tracking is not just citation compliance — it protects thinking. Keep privacy and age-appropriate source choices in mind.
