# Make the First Step Tiny

**Short summary:** Starting gets easier when the first step is tiny enough to do right now.

## Big idea

"Start the project" is too big to begin. The first step should be almost embarrassingly small — small enough that you can do it in a couple of minutes without much effort.

## Why it matters

Starting is a separate skill from finishing, and it is often the hardest part. A tiny first step lowers the barrier and can create momentum. Opening the notebook counts. Finding the pencil counts. Writing the title counts.

## Kid-friendly explanation

A big task is like a heavy door. You do not have to push the whole building — you just need to crack the door open a little. Once it is open, the next push is easier.

## Tool: Tiny First Step

> - Big task: ___
> - Tiny first step: ___
> - I can do this in less than ___ minutes.
> - After that, I can choose the next step.

## Activity: Make It Smaller

Shrink each task into a first step that takes 2 minutes or less.

- Write a report.
- Practice typing.
- Clean the kitchen.
- Build a model.
- Make a presentation.
- Study for a quiz.

For example, "Write a report" might shrink to: "Open the document and type the title."

## Discussion questions

- Why does a tiny step make starting easier?
- What is the difference between starting and finishing?
- Can a first step be too big to start?
- What tiny step helps you begin?

## Try it this week

Pick one task and make the first step so small you can do it in two minutes.

## Adult note

Respect the tiny start. Do not respond with "That's all?" Starting is often the hardest part of the whole task — praise the launch, not just the finish.
