# What Is the Job?

**Short summary:** A task gets easier when you can name what the job actually is. "Do homework" or "clean up" is often too blurry to start.

## Big idea

A clear job says **what needs to happen** and **what done looks like**. Making the job visible is the first move in getting anything done.

## Why it matters

Vague tasks are hard to start because your brain cannot picture the first step. Sometimes adults hand out blurry tasks without meaning to. Clarifying the job is not arguing — it is making the work visible so you can actually begin.

## Kid-friendly explanation

Trying to start a blurry task is like using a foggy map. You cannot see where to go, so you freeze. The first job is to clear the fog enough to see the path. "Clean your room" is foggy. "Put books on the shelf, clothes in the hamper, trash in the bin" is a map you can follow.

## Tool: the Job Clearer

> - What am I being asked to do?
> - What does done look like?
> - When does it need to be done?
> - What should I do first?
> - Who can clarify if I'm not sure?

## Activity: Blurry Job → Clear Job

Rewrite each vague job into a clear one with a visible "done" point.

- "Clean your room."
- "Work on the project."
- "Study."
- "Be ready."
- "Fix your draft."
- "Help with dinner."

For example, "Study" might become: "Do 10 practice problems from page 4, then check my answers."

## Discussion questions

- Why are blurry tasks hard to start?
- How can asking "What does done look like?" help?
- When is asking for clarification useful instead of just guessing?
- What is a task you have had that felt blurry?

## Try it this week

Before starting one task, ask: "What does done look like?"

## Adult note

Give kids visible targets. "Clean up" may secretly mean ten hidden jobs. Try: "Put books on the shelf, clothes in the hamper, and trash in the bin." Naming the job is a gift, not hand-holding.
