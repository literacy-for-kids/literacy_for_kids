# Recover After Interruptions

**Short summary:** Interruptions happen. Recovery is part of the system.

## Big idea

Getting interrupted does not mean the whole task failed. A restart point — a note to future you — makes it much easier to pick up where you left off.

## Why it matters

The hardest part of an interruption is often finding your place again, and the frustration of losing your place can make restarting even harder. A visible marker and a short restart routine handle both the practical and the emotional side.

## Kid-friendly explanation

When you stop reading, a bookmark helps you find your place again. Tasks need bookmarks too. Before you stop, leave a note so future-you knows exactly where to jump back in.

## Tool: Restart Bookmark

Before stopping, write:

> - I stopped at ___.
> - The next step is ___.
> - I need ___.
> - When I return, I will ___.

A short restart routine:

1. Take one breath.
2. Read the bookmark.
3. Do the next tiny step.

## Activity: Pause and Return

Start a small task, pause on purpose, write a restart bookmark, do something else for one minute, then return using the bookmark. Notice how much the bookmark helped.

## Discussion questions

- Why can interruptions feel so frustrating?
- What helps you find your place again?
- How is a task bookmark like a reading bookmark?
- What should future-you know before you stop?

## Try it this week

Before stopping one task, write a restart bookmark.

## Adult note

For many kids, transitions and interruptions are genuinely hard. Give a warning before a transition when possible, and help create a restart point *before* stopping rather than expecting a clean pickup later.
