# Executive Function Toolkit

Short, practical lessons that help kids plan, start, organize, prioritize, estimate time, manage distractions, recover after interruptions, track progress, and finish tasks without shame.

The other Literacy for Kids curricula explain the systems around us — technology, media, money, rules, communities, relationships, the planet, and the body. This toolkit looks at a system kids use every day: how tasks get done.

Executive function is the set of skills that helps people turn "I need to do this" into "I know what to do next." It includes planning, starting, organizing, remembering, choosing what matters, estimating time, handling distractions, and getting back on track.

Executive function helps people move from:

> "I don't know how to start."

to:

> "I know the next small step."

## What executive function skills are

Executive function skills are everyday tools for managing tasks. They help kids make the work visible, break it into parts, choose a starting point, keep track of materials, estimate time, handle distractions, restart after interruptions, and decide what "done enough" looks like.

The core ideas are simple:

- Getting things done is a **skill.**
- Starting is a **separate skill** from finishing.
- A task gets easier when you can **see it clearly.**
- A **tiny first step** makes starting easier.
- Checklists **reduce memory load.**
- Time estimates are **guesses that improve with practice.**
- Prioritizing means **choosing what matters now.**
- Distractions are **attention pulls, not moral failures.**
- Interruptions happen; **recovery is part of the system.**
- "Done enough" **depends on the purpose.**
- Asking for help, reminders, breaks, or accommodations can be **part of the plan.**
- Different brains may **need different tools.**

This is not "be more disciplined," "stop being lazy," or "be productive all the time." It is practical executive function literacy: how to make tasks visible, startable, organized, trackable, recoverable, and finishable.

## Why executive function belongs in every literacy

Every curriculum asks kids to do things:

- start a decision journal
- save files and manage digital projects
- investigate a media claim
- track spending or make a budget
- participate in civic activities
- understand rules and agreements
- practice emotional and social skills
- observe health patterns
- build environmental models or proposals

That is why this toolkit is a **shared layer** rather than another curriculum. The same task-management moves appear everywhere: name the job, picture done, make the first step tiny, gather tools, choose what matters, estimate time, protect attention, restart, and finish.

## How this connects to the other shared toolkits

This is the fifth shared toolkit, and the five work together:

- **Coping** helps kids stay regulated when tasks feel overwhelming.
- **Communication** helps kids ask for help, explain what they need, and clarify expectations.
- **Problem Solving** helps kids define what is stuck and try one safe next step.
- **Learning How to Learn** helps kids practice, remember, get feedback, and improve over time.
- **Executive Function** helps kids plan, start, organize, prioritize, recover, and finish.

Together they form a simple sequence:

1. Notice what is happening inside me.
2. Communicate clearly with other people.
3. Define the problem and try a useful next step.
4. Practice in a way that helps me improve.
5. Make the task visible, startable, and finishable.

You can explore the first four here: the [Coping Skills Toolkit](../coping-skills/intro.md), the [Communication Toolkit](../communication/intro.md), the [Problem Solving Toolkit](../problem-solving/intro.md), and the [Learning How to Learn Toolkit](../learning-how-to-learn/intro.md).

## The Start-Finish Loop

This loop appears throughout the toolkit. The full version is great for printables and older learners.

1. What is the job?
2. What does "done" look like?
3. What is the first tiny step?
4. What do I need?
5. What matters most?
6. How much time or energy might it take?
7. What could distract or interrupt me?
8. What will help me restart?
9. How will I know I'm done enough?

A shorter kid-facing version works well for quick callouts and younger learners:

> Name the job. Picture done. Make the first step tiny. Gather what you need. Pick what matters first. Try a time guess. Protect your attention. Restart if interrupted. Check "done enough."

## Who it is for

These lessons are written for children ages 8–12 and designed to be used by parents, teachers, homeschoolers, clubs, libraries, and small groups. No special training is required. Each lesson is short, no-prep, and built around conversation rather than lecture.

## A note on safety and support

:::caution Everyday task skills, not a diagnosis
This toolkit teaches everyday planning and task-management skills. It is not therapy, medical advice, disability evaluation, ADHD treatment, or a replacement for support from trusted adults, educators, caregivers, or qualified professionals. Kids should not be treated as lazy or broken when starting, focusing, organizing, or finishing is hard. If a child is consistently overwhelmed, unable to access tasks, unsafe, or severely distressed, involve a trusted adult and appropriate support. Fictional examples work fine.
:::

## How to use the toolkit

Each lesson takes about 10–20 minutes. Use one lesson on its own, mix it into another curriculum, or work through the full sequence. The printable cards are quick references kids can keep nearby.

### Suggested sequence

1. [What Is the Job?](./lesson-01-what-is-the-job.md) — make the task clear
2. [Make the First Step Tiny](./lesson-02-make-the-first-step-tiny.md) — lower the barrier to starting
3. [Use a Checklist](./lesson-03-use-a-checklist.md) — reduce memory load
4. [Estimate Time and Energy](./lesson-04-estimate-time-and-energy.md) — make better guesses about effort
5. [Prioritize What Matters](./lesson-05-prioritize-what-matters.md) — choose what matters now
6. [Handle Distractions](./lesson-06-handle-distractions.md) — notice and manage attention pulls
7. [Recover After Interruptions](./lesson-07-recover-after-interruptions.md) — get back on track without starting over emotionally
8. [Build Your Start-Finish Plan](./lesson-08-build-your-start-finish-plan.md) — create a personal task-management toolkit

Then keep the [Printable Executive Function Cards](./printables.md) handy for quick reference.

- **Environmental Systems Literacy** — manage audits, models, proposals, and long-term projects.
