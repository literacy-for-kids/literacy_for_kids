# Estimate Time and Energy

**Short summary:** Time and energy estimates are guesses that improve with practice.

## Big idea

People often guess wrong about how long something will take or how much energy it needs. That is normal. Estimates get better when you compare your guess to what actually happened.

## Why it matters

Time and energy are different. A task can be short but hard (draining), or long but easy. Knowing the difference helps you plan — and building in a little buffer time keeps a wrong guess from wrecking the whole plan.

## Kid-friendly explanation

A time estimate is like a weather forecast. The first forecast might be wrong, but looking at what actually happened makes the next forecast better. Your guesses improve every time you check them.

## Tool: Guess / Actual / Next Guess

> - I think this will take ___ minutes.
> - My energy is low / medium / high.
> - It actually took ___ minutes.
> - Next time I would guess ___.

## Activity: Time Detective

Estimate how long each simple task will take, then time it and compare.

- sharpen a few pencils
- copy three sentences
- pack a bag
- open a file and save it
- sort five objects
- clean a small area

## Discussion questions

- Why are time guesses often wrong?
- What is the difference between time and energy?
- What kind of task drains your energy fastest?
- How can knowing the actual time help next time?

## Try it this week

Pick one task. Guess how long it will take, time it, and write down the actual time.

## Adult note

Avoid using a wrong estimate as proof a child "should have known." Estimation is a skill built from feedback — the wrong guess is the raw material, not a failure.
