# Prioritize What Matters

**Short summary:** Prioritizing means choosing what matters now. It does not mean doing everything.

## Big idea

Not every task is equally important or equally urgent. Prioritizing helps you pick a starting point when there are too many things — and choosing one thing first does not mean the others do not matter.

## Why it matters

Too many tasks at once can make starting feel impossible. Sorting them into what must happen, what should happen, and what could happen turns an overwhelming pile into a clear first move.

## Kid-friendly explanation

Think of a traffic light. Some tasks are **red** — stop and do them soon. Some are **yellow** — plan them. Some are **green** — do them if there is time. Sorting by color makes the pile smaller in your mind.

## Tool: Must / Should / Could

> - **Must do:** important or time-sensitive
> - **Should do:** useful but not urgent
> - **Could do:** nice if there is time

Another way to sort — **"What matters now?"**: What is due soon? What affects other people? What helps the next step? What can wait?

## Activity: Priority Sort

Sort these example tasks into Must / Should / Could, then talk about how a different situation could change the order.

- finish a permission slip due tomorrow
- decorate a notebook
- feed a pet
- practice spelling
- reply to a friend
- organize markers
- pack for tomorrow
- fix a project slide

## Discussion questions

- Why can too many tasks make starting harder?
- How does sorting help?
- When should you ask an adult to help prioritize?
- Can two people disagree about what matters first?

## Try it this week

Make a must / should / could list for one busy afternoon.

## Adult note

Prioritizing is not obvious to kids — adults often know hidden deadlines or consequences. Share the reason: "This matters first because…" so the sorting makes sense.
