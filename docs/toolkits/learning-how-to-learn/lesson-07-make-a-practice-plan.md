# Make a Practice Plan

**Short summary:** Practice works better when you know what, how, when, and how you will check progress.

## Big idea

"Practice more" is too vague to follow. A good practice plan is small and specific, aims at the fuzzy part, and says when you will come back. A plan that is easy to start beats a perfect plan nobody uses.

## Why it matters

Vague goals are hard to begin, so they get put off. A tiny, specific plan tells you exactly what to do next, which makes starting easy. Good plans also leave room for help, breaks, reminders, and feedback — and they can change when they are not working.

## Kid-friendly explanation

A practice plan is a recipe for getting better. "Cook food" is too vague to follow — you need to know what dish, which ingredients, and the steps. "Practice math" is vague the same way. "Practice 6 times-tables with flashcards for 5 minutes, then check" is a recipe you can actually follow.

## Tool: the Practice Plan Card

> - **Skill:** What am I working on?
> - **Fuzzy part:** What exactly is hard?
> - **Practice method:** How will I practice it?
> - **Time:** How long?
> - **Next practice:** When will I come back?
> - **How I will check:** How will I know it helped?
> - **Who can help:** Who or what can support me?

## Activity: Plan Makeover

Turn each vague plan into a small, specific one using the card above.

- "Study more."
- "Practice piano."
- "Get better at typing."
- "Learn about elections."
- "Understand the water cycle."
- "Stop forgetting vocabulary."

## Discussion questions

- Why is "practice more" hard to follow?
- How small should a practice step be to make it easy to start?
- What makes a plan easy to begin?
- When should a plan change?

## Try it this week

Make one tiny practice plan for a real skill and use it once. Keep it small enough that you actually do it.

## Adult note

Keep practice plans realistic and kid-sized. A tiny plan that happens is worth far more than an ambitious plan that does not. If a plan keeps failing, that is information — shrink it, not the child.
