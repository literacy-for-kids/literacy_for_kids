# Small Practice Over Time

**Short summary:** Short practice sessions spread out over time usually work better than one giant cram session.

## Big idea

Coming back to something a little at a time builds a stronger, longer-lasting memory than trying to learn it all at once. Small practice is also easier to start.

## Why it matters

Cramming can feel like it works because the information is fresh for a few hours. But it fades fast. Practice that is spread out — with breaks and sleep in between — gives your brain time to make the memory stick. Small practice also feels less overwhelming, so you are more likely to actually do it.

## Kid-friendly explanation

Imagine watering a plant. Dumping a whole bucket on it once does not help it grow — most of the water runs off. Watering a little bit over several days works far better. Learning grows the same way: a few short visits beat one giant flood.

Practice can be short. Five or ten minutes counts. Breaks and sleep are part of how learning settles in.

## Tool: the Tiny Practice Plan

> - What will I practice?
> - For how long?
> - When will I come back to it?
> - How will I check what I remember?

## Activity: Three Tiny Visits

1. Choose one simple skill or set of facts.
2. Practice it for 2 minutes now.
3. Take a break and practice 2 more minutes a bit later.
4. Practice 2 minutes again tomorrow or next session.
5. If tomorrow is not possible during the lesson, make a plan card so it happens later.

Talk about how the second and third visits felt easier than the first.

## Discussion questions

- Why does cramming sometimes feel like it works?
- Why might small practice be easier to start?
- What is one skill that gets better when you come back to it again and again?
- How can breaks and sleep help learning?

## Try it this week

Pick one thing and practice it for about 5 minutes on three different days. Notice whether it feels easier by the third day.

## Adult note

Help kids make practice small enough to actually start. "Five minutes now and five minutes tomorrow" usually beats a giant study plan that never happens. Protect breaks and sleep — they are not the opposite of learning, they are part of it.
