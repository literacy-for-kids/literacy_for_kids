# Practice Beats Rereading

**Short summary:** Looking at information again can feel like learning, but practicing remembering usually helps more.

## Big idea

There is a difference between **looking practice** (reading it again) and **remembering practice** (pulling the answer out of your own brain). Remembering practice feels harder, and that harder feeling is a sign it is working.

## Why it matters

Rereading is not bad, but it can trick you. When something looks familiar, your brain says, "I know this." Familiar is not the same as learned. The only way to find out what actually stuck is to try to remember it without looking.

## Kid-friendly explanation

Looking at the answer is like watching someone else ride a bike. It looks easy, and you feel like you get it. Remembering practice is like getting on the bike and pedaling yourself. It wobbles at first — but that is how you actually learn to ride.

Ways to do remembering practice:

- flashcards
- explaining an idea from memory
- writing what you remember on a blank page
- doing practice problems without peeking

## Tool: Look, Cover, Try, Check

> 1. **Look** at the idea.
> 2. **Cover** it.
> 3. **Try** to say, write, draw, or use it from memory.
> 4. **Check** what was right and what needs another try.

Then practice the fuzzy part again.

## Activity: Reread vs. Remember

1. Take a tiny paragraph or a short list of words.
2. First, reread it twice.
3. Now cover it and recall as much as you can.
4. Compare how those two felt. Rereading felt smooth; remembering felt harder.
5. Which one showed you what you actually knew? Practice the fuzzy part.

## Discussion questions

- Why does rereading feel easier than remembering?
- Why does remembering practice feel harder?
- Which one tells you more about what you actually know?
- When might rereading still be useful?

## Try it this week

After you read something you want to learn, close the page and say three things you remember. Then check. Practice whatever you missed.

## Adult note

Do not ban rereading — pair it with recall. The point is not "make it harder for the sake of it." The point is that recall shows what really stuck, so practice lands where it is needed instead of on things that only look familiar.
