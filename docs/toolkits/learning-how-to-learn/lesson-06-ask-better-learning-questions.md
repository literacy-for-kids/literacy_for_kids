# Ask Better Learning Questions

**Short summary:** Clear questions get better help than "I don't get it."

## Big idea

"I don't get it" is a real signal, but it is too broad for a helper to act on. A better question points to the exact part that is stuck — and pointing at the stuck part is a skill you can practice.

## Why it matters

When you can name what you understand, where you got stuck, and what you already tried, helpers can help you fast. Asking for help this way is not weakness — it is one of the smartest learning moves there is. This move overlaps with the Communication and Problem Solving toolkits.

## Kid-friendly explanation

Imagine telling a map app, "I'm lost." It cannot help until it knows where you are and where you want to go. A helper is the same. "I don't get fractions" is like "I'm lost." "I understand how to make halves, but I'm stuck when the bottom numbers are different" gives the helper a starting point.

## Tool: the Better Learning Question Frame

> - I am trying to learn ___.
> - I understand ___.
> - I am stuck on ___.
> - I tried ___.
> - Can you help me with ___?

## Activity: Upgrade the Question

Rewrite each broad statement into a better learning question.

- "I don't get fractions."
- "This article makes no sense."
- "My code doesn't work."
- "I don't understand the rule."
- "I can't remember this."

For example: "My code doesn't work" might become: "I'm trying to make the box turn red. I understand how to change color, but I'm stuck because nothing happens when I click. I tried moving the color line. Can you help me figure out why the click isn't working?"

## Discussion questions

- Why is "I don't get it" sometimes hard for helpers to answer?
- How does naming the stuck part change the help you get?
- What is one question you could ask even when you feel embarrassed?
- How can a helper respond kindly to a question?

## Try it this week

The next time you are stuck, use: "I understand ___, but I'm stuck on ___."

## Adult note

Reward specific questions warmly. If a child says "I don't get it," do not sigh — scaffold: "What part makes sense so far?" That models the very skill this lesson teaches and keeps asking for help safe.
