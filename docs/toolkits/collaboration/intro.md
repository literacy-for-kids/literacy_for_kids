# Collaboration Toolkit

Short, practical lessons that help kids define shared goals, choose roles, share work fairly, check in, disagree respectfully, repair group friction, finish together, and give credit without shame.

The other Literacy for Kids curricula explain the systems around us — technology, media, money, rules, communities, relationships, the planet, and the body. This toolkit looks at a system kids use all the time: shared work with other people.

Collaboration is how people turn "we need to do this together" into "we know what we are making, who is doing what, how to check in, and how to repair if it gets messy."

Collaboration helps people move from:

> "Group work is confusing and someone always gets mad."

to:

> "We know the shared goal, the roles, the check-in plan, and the next step."

## What collaboration skills are

Collaboration skills are everyday tools for shared work. They help kids name the shared goal, define what done looks like, choose roles, share work fairly, check in without taking over, disagree without attacking, repair group friction, finish together, and give credit.

The core ideas are simple:

- Collaboration is a **skill.**
- Working together does **not** mean everyone does the same thing.
- A shared goal helps people **aim at the same target.**
- Roles make work **visible.**
- Fair work is **discussed, not guessed.**
- Check-ins help groups **catch problems early.**
- Disagreement is **not the same as disrespect.**
- Repair matters when group work **gets messy.**
- Finishing together includes **credit, cleanup, and reflection.**
- Asking for adult help can be **part of safe collaboration.**
- Different people may **contribute in different ways.**
- No one should be expected to collaborate through **bullying, coercion, exclusion, or unsafe behavior.**

This is not "just be nice," "always agree," or "let one person do all the work." It is practical collaboration literacy: how shared work becomes visible, fair, respectful, coordinated, repairable, and finishable.

## Why collaboration belongs in every literacy

Every curriculum eventually asks kids to work with or around other people:

- make group decisions
- build digital projects
- analyze media messages together
- talk about money choices
- participate in civic life
- understand agreements and rules
- practice emotional and social skills
- discuss health routines and trusted adults
- investigate environmental systems and proposals

That is why this toolkit is a **shared layer** rather than another curriculum. The same collaboration moves appear everywhere: name the shared goal, define done, choose roles, share work fairly, check in, disagree respectfully, repair, and finish together.

## How this connects to the other shared toolkits

This is the sixth shared toolkit, and the six work together:

- **Coping** helps kids stay regulated when group work feels stressful.
- **Communication** helps kids ask, explain, listen, clarify, and repair.
- **Problem Solving** helps groups define what is stuck and try a useful next step.
- **Learning How to Learn** helps groups use feedback and improve over time.
- **Executive Function** helps groups make tasks visible, startable, trackable, and finishable.
- **Collaboration** helps kids coordinate shared work, roles, fairness, check-ins, disagreement, repair, and credit.

Together they form a simple sequence:

1. Notice what is happening inside me.
2. Communicate clearly with other people.
3. Define the problem and try a useful next step.
4. Practice in a way that helps me improve.
5. Make tasks visible, startable, and finishable.
6. Work with others toward a shared goal.

You can explore the first five here: the [Coping Skills Toolkit](../coping-skills/intro.md), the [Communication Toolkit](../communication/intro.md), the [Problem Solving Toolkit](../problem-solving/intro.md), the [Learning How to Learn Toolkit](../learning-how-to-learn/intro.md), and the [Executive Function Toolkit](../executive-function/intro.md).

## The Collaboration Loop

This loop appears throughout the toolkit. The full version is great for printables and older learners.

1. What are we making or doing together?
2. What does done look like?
3. What roles do we need?
4. How will we share the work fairly?
5. How will we check in?
6. What will we do if we disagree?
7. What will we do if the plan gets messy?
8. How will we finish and give credit?

A shorter kid-facing version works well for quick callouts and younger learners:

> Name the shared goal. Picture done. Choose roles. Share the work. Check in. Disagree respectfully. Repair messes. Finish together.

## Who it is for

These lessons are written for children ages 8–12 and designed to be used by parents, teachers, homeschoolers, clubs, libraries, and small groups. No special training is required. Each lesson is short, no-prep, and built around conversation rather than lecture.

## A note on safety and support

:::caution Everyday collaboration skills, not unsafe conflict management
This toolkit teaches everyday collaboration skills. It is not therapy, bullying intervention, conflict mediation, legal advice, or a replacement for support from trusted adults, educators, caregivers, or qualified professionals. Kids should not be expected to manage unsafe, coercive, bullying, discriminatory, or threatening group situations alone. If group work feels unsafe, targeted, or too big, involve a trusted adult. Fictional examples work fine.
:::

## How to use the toolkit

Each lesson takes about 10–20 minutes. Use one lesson on its own, mix it into another curriculum, or work through the full sequence. The printable cards are quick references kids can keep nearby.

### Suggested sequence

1. [What Are We Making Together?](./lesson-01-what-are-we-making-together.md) — define the shared goal
2. [Roles Make Work Visible](./lesson-02-roles-make-work-visible.md) — choose jobs people can actually see
3. [Share the Work Fairly](./lesson-03-share-the-work-fairly.md) — talk about fairness before resentment grows
4. [Check In Without Taking Over](./lesson-04-check-in-without-taking-over.md) — notice progress without bossing people around
5. [Disagree and Keep Building](./lesson-05-disagree-and-keep-building.md) — use disagreement to improve the work
6. [Repair When Group Work Gets Messy](./lesson-06-repair-when-group-work-gets-messy.md) — recover from confusion, hurt, or unfairness
7. [Finish Together](./lesson-07-finish-together.md) — give credit, clean up, and reflect
8. [Build Your Collaboration Plan](./lesson-08-build-your-collaboration-plan.md) — create a personal and shared group-work toolkit

Then keep the [Printable Collaboration Cards](./printables.md) handy for quick reference.

## How each curriculum can use the tools

- **Decision Literacy** — make group choices, compare options, and review decisions together.
- **Computer Literacy** — build digital projects with roles, files, debugging, and shared versions.
- **Media Literacy** — plan group media messages, source checks, scripts, drafts, and feedback.
- **Financial Literacy** — discuss shared resources, tradeoffs, fairness, and group goals.
- **Civic Literacy** — practice roles, proposals, meetings, responsibilities, and shared action.
- **Legal Literacy** — understand agreements, roles, rules, evidence, and group responsibilities.
- **Emotional & Social Literacy** — practice boundaries, group dynamics, repair, and inclusion.
- **Health Systems Literacy** — discuss routines, trusted adults, family and team habits, and support.
- **Environmental Systems Literacy** — run audits, models, proposals, and community projects together.

Each of those curricula includes its own short "Collaboration Skills" page and "Collaboration Moment" callouts that link back to the tools here.
