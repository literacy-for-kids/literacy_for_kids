# Share the Work Fairly

**Short summary:** Fair work is discussed, not guessed. Fair does not always mean identical.

## Big idea

Equal means everyone does the same amount. Fair means the group considers time, effort, skills, access, needs, and support. A group can be fair without being identical — but it cannot be fair silently.

## Why it matters

Two things quietly wreck group work: one person carrying the whole group, and one person being left with nothing to contribute. Both feel terrible, and both usually happen without anyone deciding them out loud. If work feels unfair, the group needs a check-in — not a grudge.

## Kid-friendly explanation

If a group is hiking, fair does not always mean every backpack has exactly the same items. Someone smaller might carry less weight; someone with the map has a different job. But fair definitely means nobody should be crushed under everything while someone else carries nothing.

## Tool: the Fair Work Check

> - What work needs to happen?
> - Who has which part?
> - Does anyone have too much?
> - Does anyone have too little or no way to contribute?
> - What support is needed?
> - What should change?

## Activity: Fair or Not?

Discuss each fictional example. Is it fair? What would make it fairer?

- One kid does all the slides because they are fastest.
- One kid wants to help but nobody gives them a role.
- Two kids research while one kid decorates.
- A group assigns the hardest speaking role to someone who is terrified of speaking.
- A group changes roles after a check-in.

## Discussion questions

- Why is fairness sometimes different from equal?
- How can a group notice unfair work early?
- What can someone say if they feel overloaded?
- What can someone say if they feel left out?

## Try it this week

During one group activity, ask: "Does anyone have too much, too little, or need support?"

## Adult note

Fairness includes access and support. A child who needs a different role, tool, or amount of help is not cheating. Do not let "fair" become a way to shame or exclude — and do not let "team player" become pressure to accept unfairness.
