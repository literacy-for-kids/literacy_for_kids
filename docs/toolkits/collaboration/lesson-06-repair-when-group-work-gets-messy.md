# Repair When Group Work Gets Messy

**Short summary:** Groups can recover from confusion, unfairness, hurt, or mistakes by naming what happened and choosing the next repair step.

## Big idea

Group work gets messy sometimes — that is normal, not proof the group failed. Repair is not humiliation, and it is not pretending nothing happened. It asks: what happened, who was affected, and what needs to change?

## Why it matters

An unrepaired mess does not stay the same size — it grows. The person who got talked over stops contributing. The person who got stuck stops asking. Naming the mess early, without blame, is how groups keep working together after something goes wrong. Some situations are bigger than everyday repair; those need a trusted adult.

## Kid-friendly explanation

When string gets tangled, yanking usually makes it worse. Repair means slowing down enough to untangle one part at a time — find the knot, loosen it gently, and keep the string usable.

## Tool: the Group Repair Check

> - What happened?
> - Who was affected?
> - What does the group need now?
> - What should change?
> - Who can help if this is too big?

## Activity: Repair the Group

For each fictional scenario, choose one small repair step using the check:

- One person took over the slides.
- Two people argued and stopped working.
- Someone was left out of the role list.
- A group missed a deadline because everyone thought someone else was doing it.
- A joke hurt someone.

## Discussion questions

- Why is repair hard?
- What is the difference between repair and blame?
- When does a group need adult help?
- What repair step feels small enough to start?

## Try it this week

If a shared task gets messy, ask: "What needs to change so the group can keep going safely?"

## Adult note

Do not force public apologies or personal disclosure. Support repair with safety, privacy, and clear next steps. Bullying, exclusion, and targeting are not "group messes" for kids to repair alone — they need adult involvement.
