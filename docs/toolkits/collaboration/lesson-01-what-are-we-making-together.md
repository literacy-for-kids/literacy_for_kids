# What Are We Making Together?

**Short summary:** A group works better when everyone knows the shared goal. Most group messes start because people think they are making different things.

## Big idea

A shared goal says what the group is trying to make, decide, solve, or finish. When everyone can say the goal out loud, roles, fairness, and progress all get easier.

## Why it matters

Groups often get messy not because people are lazy or mean, but because they are quietly aiming at different targets. "Do the project" is not a shared goal. "Make a 3-minute presentation that teaches three ways to check a source" is — it tells everyone what done looks like.

## Kid-friendly explanation

If everyone on a team is aiming at a different target, the arrows go everywhere — and everyone thinks the *other* people missed. A shared goal gives the group one target. Then even people doing different jobs are aiming at the same spot.

## Tool: the Shared Goal Frame

> - We are working together to ___.
> - Done looks like ___.
> - This is for ___.
> - The most important part is ___.
> - We will know it worked when ___.

## Activity: Blurry Group Goal → Clear Shared Goal

Rewrite each blurry goal into a clear shared goal using the frame.

- "Do the poster."
- "Work on the project."
- "Make something about recycling."
- "Prepare for the debate."
- "Fix the group presentation."
- "Plan the class idea."

For example, "Make something about recycling" might become: "Make a poster that shows our class three things that actually belong in the recycling bin."

## Discussion questions

- Why do groups get confused when the goal is blurry?
- How can a shared goal reduce arguments?
- What is the difference between a task and a shared goal?
- When should a group ask an adult to clarify the goal?

## Try it this week

Before one group activity, ask: "What are we making or doing together?"

## Adult note

Do not assume kids share your picture of the task. Say what done looks like. A clear shared goal prevents many group conflicts before they start — it is one of the cheapest fairness tools there is.
