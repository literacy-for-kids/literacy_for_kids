# Literacy for Kids

Literacy for Kids is a collection of open-source curricula designed for children ages 8–12. Each curriculum teaches a foundational life literacy that helps children understand the systems shaping modern life.

Every day, children interact with complex systems — technology, media, money, decisions, and civic life — but schools rarely explain how those systems work. These curricula provide that understanding through short, discussion-based lessons.

## The Curricula

This project includes nine independent curricula:

- [🧠 Decision Literacy for Kids](https://decision.literacy-for-kids.com/) — reasoning and choices
- [💻 Computer Literacy for Kids](https://computer.literacy-for-kids.com/) — understanding technology
- [📰 Media Literacy for Kids](https://media.literacy-for-kids.com/) — understanding information
- [💰 Financial Literacy for Kids](https://financial.literacy-for-kids.com/) — understanding money
- [🏛 Civic Literacy for Kids](https://civic.literacy-for-kids.com/) — understanding governance
- [🧩 Emotional & Social Literacy for Kids](https://emotional.literacy-for-kids.com/) — understanding self and others
- [⚖️ Legal Literacy for Kids](https://legal.literacy-for-kids.com/) — understanding rules and agreements
- [🌍 Environmental Systems Literacy](https://environmental.literacy-for-kids.com/) — understanding Earth systems
- [🩺 Health Systems Literacy for Kids](https://health.literacy-for-kids.com/) — understanding the body

## Shared toolkits

Alongside the nine curricula, the project includes shared toolkits — short, practical skill sets that support every literacy rather than belonging to just one. The first is the [Coping Skills Toolkit](./toolkits/coping-skills/intro.md): everyday tools that help kids notice stress signals, pause before reacting, calm their body, check their thoughts, ask for help, and recover after hard moments. Because every literacy involves real feelings in real situations, these skills strengthen the work across the whole ecosystem.

## How they can be used

The curricula can be:

- taught independently
- used together
- taught sequentially
- integrated into classroom, homeschool, or after-school programs

Each lesson typically takes 10–20 minutes and emphasizes discussion, exploration, and critical thinking rather than lectures.
